Before you act on this

  1. Survivorship bias. The universe is today's index membership. Names that broke their 200 DMA and never recovered were deleted from the index and are invisible here. Every forward-return figure below is biased upward as a result.
  2. The rarity filter selects for stocks that have already compounded. Intel, GE, Cisco and Nokia all had this profile once. Each eventually broke the 200 DMA and stayed broken — and the break was the signal that the story had changed, not that the stock was cheap.
  3. Prior breaks is the real sample size. Four breaks in ten years means forward returns rest on four observations. Those rows are marked THIN and their statistics are anecdote.
  4. Nothing here reads a financial statement. The screen cannot tell a quality business in a macro drawdown from one that is de-rating. The slope column hints at it. It does not settle it.
  5. Prices are split- and dividend-adjusted, so history shifts retroactively after each dividend and an old scan will not reproduce exactly.
